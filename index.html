Masaaki Yuasa

  • March 16, 1965 / Fukuoka, Japan

      • Masaaki Yuasa is a Japanese animation director, screenwriter, and animator known for his imaginative storytelling and unique visual style. Born on March 16, 1965, in Fukuoka, Japan, he has become an important figure in modern animation.
      Yuasa experiments with shapes, colors, movement, and perspective. His characters can stretch, change shape, and move in unexpected ways. These techniques make his work energetic, expressive, and visually distinctive.
      Through his films and television series, Yuasa explores themes such as identity, freedom, youth, love, and personal growth. His work shows that animation is an art form that can challenge our imagination and change how we see the world.

    Section 1：Early Life and Career

    Masaaki Yuasa was born in Fukuoka, Japan, in 1965. He studied fine arts at Kyushu Sangyo University before beginning his professional career in animation. In his early years, he worked as an animator and contributed to popular Japanese television series, including Chibi Maruko-chan and Crayon Shin-chan.​ These early experiences helped him understand how animation could communicate emotions through movement, facial expressions, timing, and visual design.
    However, Yuasa wanted to explore more personal and experimental ways of telling stories.​ In 2004, he made his feature-film directorial debut with Mind Game. The film combines unusual character designs, rapidly changing visual styles, and imaginative storytelling. It received the Grand Prize in the Animation Division at the 8th Japan Media Arts Festival, bringing significant recognition to Yuasa's work.

    Section 2:A Unique Visual Style

    One of the most recognizable features of Masaaki Yuasa's work is his unconventional approach to animation. Instead of keeping characters and environments within realistic proportions, he often stretches bodies, distorts perspectives, and changes shapes to express emotions and ideas.​
    Color also plays an important role in his visual storytelling. Bright colors can create excitement and freedom, while darker tones can communicate fear, confusion, or loneliness. His use of color helps audiences understand the mood of a scene even before the characters speak.​
    Movement is another essential part of Yuasa's style. His characters often move in exaggerated and unpredictable ways, making action feel energetic and spontaneous. In Ping Pong the Animation, for example, the visual style reflects the characters' personalities, confidence, and emotional struggles.

    Section 3:Major Works

    • Work 1：The Tatami Galaxy (2010)

      • The Tatami Galaxy follows a university student who dreams of having the perfect college life. As he imagines how different choices might have changed his future, he discovers the importance of friendship, personal responsibility, and self-acceptance. The series combines fast-paced narration, surreal situations, and distinctive visual compositions to create an imaginative portrait of youth and regret.
      The series won the Grand Prize in the Animation Division at the 14th Japan Media Arts Festival. Its unusual storytelling, expressive colors, and creative visual design make it one of Yuasa's most distinctive works.

    • Work 2：Ping Pong the Animation (2014)

      • Ping Pong the Animation follows a group of young table-tennis players with different personalities, ambitions, and fears. Through their competitions and personal struggles, the series explores talent, hard work, friendship, and the pressure to succeed. Its energetic animation and unconventional character designs express the intensity of sports while revealing the emotional challenges behind each player's journey.
      The series received a Jury Selection distinction in the Animation Division at the 18th Japan Media Arts Festival. Its dynamic movement, expressive character designs, and emotional storytelling demonstrate how animation can transform a sports story into a powerful human drama.

    • Work 3：Night Is Short, Walk on Girl (2017)

      • Night Is Short, Walk on Girl follows a young woman as she explores Kyoto during an extraordinary night filled with unexpected encounters and bizarre adventures. Along the way, ordinary streets become dreamlike spaces where imagination and reality blend together. Through its playful humor and colorful visual style, the film celebrates curiosity, spontaneity, romance, and the excitement of discovering new experiences.
      The film won the Best Animation Film award at the 41st Japan Academy Film Prize in 2018. Its surreal imagery, lively pacing, and imaginative portrayal of a single night make it a memorable example of Yuasa's creative storytelling.

    • Work 4：DEVILMAN crybaby (2018)

      • DEVILMAN crybaby presents a darker side of Masaaki Yuasa's storytelling. The series follows Akira Fudo, a kind-hearted young man who gains demonic powers while trying to protect humanity. As fear and violence spread, the story explores prejudice, human nature, friendship, and the conflict between compassion and cruelty. Its bold imagery and intense atmosphere create a challenging reflection on society and humanity.
      The series received a Jury Selection distinction in the Animation Division at the 22nd Japan Media Arts Festival. Its experimental visuals, emotional intensity, and bold reinterpretation of a classic manga demonstrate Yuasa's willingness to explore difficult themes through animation.

    • Work 5：Keep Your Hands Off Eizouken! (2020)

      • Keep Your Hands Off Eizouken! follows three high school girls who form an animation club to bring their imaginative ideas to life. Midori designs fantastic worlds, Tsubame focuses on character animation, and Kanamori manages the practical side of their projects. Together, they overcome creative and production challenges while discovering the joy of making animation. The series celebrates imagination, teamwork, and the passion behind artistic creation.
      The series won the Grand Prize in the Animation Division at the 24th Japan Media Arts Festival. Its imaginative visual sequences, humorous storytelling, and detailed portrayal of animation production make it a joyful tribute to creativity and the art of filmmaking.

    Section 4:Influence and Legacy

    Masaaki Yuasa has made an important contribution to contemporary animation by showing that artistic experimentation and storytelling can work together. His films and television series challenge conventional ideas about character design, movement, color, and visual composition.
    In 2013, Yuasa co-founded Science SARU with producer Eunyoung Choi. The studio became known for its distinctive animation projects and creative approach to production. Through his work with the studio, Yuasa helped demonstrate that animation could combine different techniques and visual influences to create original experiences.
    His work has also received recognition at international film festivals and Japanese cultural awards. Films such as Mind Game and Lu Over the Wall, as well as the television series The Tatami Galaxy, have received major awards or critical recognition.
    Yuasa's legacy is not limited to the films he has directed. His creative choices encourage audiences and aspiring artists to question established rules, experiment with new ideas, and discover their own ways of expressing themselves. He reminds us that animation has almost unlimited possibilities when artists are willing to take creative risks.
